# Draft cover letter — for author review; not sent

Dear PLOS ONE Editors,

Please consider the Research Article **“Longitudinal motor-imagery EEG decoding:
Frozen baselines, supervised recalibration, and task-measurement reliability.”**

This secondary analysis uses 18 published participant identities across six
sessions from Kumar2024. The original study already investigated fixed/adaptive
common spatial patterns and first-run recalibration. Our narrower contribution
separates classifier-only from full spatial-filter/classifier updating with the
same 19/20 current-session labels and examines whether available task measurements
justify an update decision.

The original primary 12-participant contrast favored full refitting over frozen
decoding by 7.42 percentage points (95% participant-bootstrap interval 2.49–12.48).
The secondary equal-label-budget contrast favored full refitting over
classifier-only updating by 5.47 points (2.17–8.91), without promotion to a primary
endpoint. All fixed neural seeds, negative results and collapsed predictions
remain reported. Weak independent source-query EEGNet performance limits a
cross-session-drift explanation. New QC sensitivity preserves missing-class
limitations; event tracing qualifies the endpoint as cue-anchored task-label
decoding, not isolated sustained imagery. Prior test exposure remains explicit.
No validated adaptive gate, skill-retention effect or clinical efficacy is claimed.

The review materials provide scientific methods, a separate audit appendix and
machine-readable saved-result reproduction. Original-study ethics and consent
are distinguished from the pending institutional determination for this secondary
analysis. Specific license activation and final author approval remain pending;
this draft does not assert completed submission eligibility.
