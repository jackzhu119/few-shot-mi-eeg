10   Abstract

11   Supervised recalibration can update a brain–computer interface classifier, its spatial filters, or both.

12   We compared these choices in 18 participant records across six sessions of Kumar2024 (NEMAR

13   nm000177/v1.0.5). A frozen protocol separated six development participants from a predefined 12-

14   participant primary cohort. Source-only CSP/LDA was compared with classifier-only recalibration

15   and full CSP/LDA refitting; the two update methods used the same 19 or 20 current-session support

16   labels. Always-frozen EEGNet retained three fixed seeds. The primary full-refit-minus-frozen con-

17   trast was 7.42 percentage points (95% participant-bootstrap interval 2.49–12.48). The secondary

18   same-label-budget full-refit-minus-LDA-only contrast was 5.47 points (2.17–8.91), whereas LDA-

19   only recalibration versus frozen decoding remained uncertain. Three of the 12 participants had de-

20   creases of 0.20–5.82 points. An exploratory amplitude-QC analysis retained complete five-session

21   endpoints for ten participants, with a full-minus-frozen contrast of 5.96 points (0.99–11.24); missing

22   classes and conditional selection limit this comparison. In the primary cohort, EEGNet source-query

23   balanced accuracy was 52.92% and target BA 51.58%. Weak discrimination was therefore already

24   present within the source session; the target score alone does not identify the contribution of cross-

25   session drift. Reliability probes did not establish a useful adaptation gate. The documented export

26   convention anchors five-second windows at the visual cue, but their trial-level phase composition

27   was not reconstructed. Full refitting improved the cohort average under the specified support-label

28   budget, with substantial individual variation. Prior test exposure makes the follow-up analyses ex-

29   ploratory. The findings concern released task-label windows, rather than isolated sustained imagery,

30   human skill retention or clinical efficacy.

31   Introduction

32   Longitudinal motor-imagery brain–computer interface (BCI) decoding must account for changes in

33   recording distributions and in the relation between signals and task labels. Nonstationarity, limited

34   calibration data and evaluation design remain recurring challenges [1]. Common spatial patterns

35   (CSP) and linear discriminant analysis (LDA) offer a conventional setting in which spatial repre-

36   sentation and classification can be updated separately [2,3]. A current-session label budget may

37   improve decoding, but it does not establish that every participant or session benefits from updating.


38   The acquisition study used incremental covariance recentering with a Riemannian minimum-

39   distance-to-mean classifier.   Its personally adjusted recentering framework added supervised

40   class-prototype updates during the first run of each session; a separate pseudo-online analysis

41   extended recentering to CSP and a linear discriminant classifier [4,5]. These procedures establish

42   important precedents for adaptation and session-start calibration, but differ from refitting CSP and

43   LDA on the same small labeled support set.


44   Our primary comparison asks how much full refitting improves decoding over a completely frozen

45   CSP/LDA pipeline when approximately 20 current-session labels are available. This total contrast

46   combines access to support labels with updates to both stages. The secondary comparison, full refit-

47   ting versus LDA-only recalibration, more directly addresses the additional contribution of updating

48   spatial filters because the two methods use identical labels. We retain this predefined hierarchy

49   rather than promote the secondary comparison after seeing its result. A further question is whether

50   the available task measurements can support reliable decisions about when to update.


51   Distribution alignment is a related but different strategy. Euclidean alignment and Riemannian

52   transfer frameworks reduce between-domain mismatch before or within decoding [6,7]. Such ap-

53   proaches are relevant alternatives, not baselines that were implemented here. A covariance distance

54   or task-score measurement alone neither reproduces those methods nor establishes that a policy


                                                      3
55   based on it improves decoding. Likewise, decoder accuracy is not interchangeable with a user’s

56   mental-imagery skill [8]. This secondary analysis evaluates stored task labels rather than the origi-

57   nal study’s online interventions or acquired skills.


58   Earlier work examined unsupervised adaptation of LDA in motor-imagery BCIs [9]. EEG data-

59   space adaptation instead estimated supervised or unsupervised channel-space transformations be-

60   fore applying an existing CSP/LDA decoder; one evaluated setting used the first 20 target trials for

61   adaptation [10]. These methods update different components and were not implemented as com-

62   parators here.


63   EEGNet supplies a compact neural reference [11], but identical query trials do not equalize source-

64   validation labels, target support or optimization. We retain every fixed seed and class-collapsed

65   outcome and examine saved learning histories to interpret this small-source setting. We also trace

66   the exported events to their task semantics: a complete data window is not automatically a complete

67   imagery phase. The study contributes a component-level baseline comparison and an assessment of

68   whether task measurements are adequate for individual update decisions. The reliability evidence

69   was insufficient to validate such a decision rule.

85   Exported windows, task-phase meaning and chronological partition

86   The frozen analysis identified 7,200 exported left/right event identities and used five-second inter-

87   vals beginning at their annotated onset. Forty-four intervals ended at the recording boundary before

88   five seconds; they were preserved and excluded without padding or replacement, leaving 7,156

89   complete windows. Completeness here refers to available recorded samples, not to five seconds of

90   continuous motor imagery.


91   The original author-supplied codebook defines 769/770 as left/right cue markers and distinct

92   7691/7701 markers as task-period starts [13]. The MOABB Kumar adapter [14] maps 769/770 to

93   left/right and declares [0,5] seconds; the derivative’s class annotations and fixed/clipped durations

94   agree with this documented export convention. The precise historical converter commit and

95   the original trial-level marker arrays were not independently replayed. The original Methods

96   describe a 1.5-second cue preceding feedback, with an offline task duration of five seconds and

97   online feedback lasting at most seven seconds, followed by result and rest. These sources support

98   a cue-anchored task-label export convention; they do not independently certify the phase

99   alignment of every analyzed trial. A nominal cue-anchored window includes the cue interval and



                                                          5
100   at most the subsequent 3.5 seconds; early online termination could introduce later phases. Their

101   trial-level proportions cannot be measured without the original marker sequence and a verified

102   clock mapping. We did not reconstruct these phases from labels or alter any window.


103   All 44 short intervals occur on the last event of an online recording and stop at the audited EDF end.

104   This is a recording-boundary relationship, not evidence that exactly 44 tasks ended early. Neither

105   variable task duration by itself nor this boundary check establishes a sample-indexing error. The

106   original results are retained as decoding of the released task-label windows. S1 Text gives the event

107   evidence and the minimum requirements for a future phase-specific amendment.


108   We inspected all 360 retained event tables and the saved EDF-annotation checks. They contain the

109   left/right annotation stream, without separate task-start or task-end fields. The complete NEMAR

110   manifest also publicly lists 360 original bar-task GDF files. Their embedded event arrays and their

111   trial/sample mapping to the derivative were not parsed or validated in this analysis. Thus the missing

112   phase information concerns the analyzed derivative, rather than a demonstrated absence from the

113   original public distribution (S1 Text, S7).


114   Development identities were sub-1, 4, 7, 10, 13 and 16; the remaining 12 formed the fixed primary

115   cohort, selected by numeric order rather than performance. In session 0, runs 0/1 supplied 40 source

116   TRAIN windows, run 2 supplied 20 source validation windows and run 3 supplied source query. In

117   each of five later sessions, run 0 supplied support and the remaining whole runs supplied query. Up-

118   dates used source TRAIN plus current-session support without accumulating future or earlier target

119   labels. Partial exclusions left 19/20 support events per target session (1,786 total) and 4,290 query

120   events: 360 source and 3,930 target. Thirty partial query events remained excluded. Derivative

121   timestamp order was checked; absolute calendar accuracy was not independently established.

122   Preprocessing and fixed amplitude-QC flag

123   Each event independently underwent common-average referencing across 22 channels, fourth-order

124   8–30 Hz Butterworth zero-phase filtering and polyphase resampling from 512 to 128 Hz, yielding

125   22 × 640 float32 values in volts. These epoch-local operations learned no held-out statistics. Zero-

126   phase filtering defines an offline analysis; it neither proves real-time operation nor removes a cue

127   or feedback contribution. Native CPz reference remains a metadata declaration.


128   The existing 250 µV maximum raw channel peak-to-peak rule flagged 1,512/7,156 complete win-

129   dows. Every flagged complete window remained in the primary analysis. The rule is an amplitude

130   flag, not expert artifact certification. Signal timing and non-neural contributions are separate quali-

131   fications. No threshold or preprocessing parameter was selected from the new sensitivity results.



132   Decoders and information budgets

133   The CPU pipeline used four-component CSP with Ledoit–Wolf regularization, log mean-square fea-

134   tures and norm_trace=False, followed by shrinkage LDA (solver=lsqr, automatic shrinkage).

135   One prespecified configuration was used. Source-only CSP and LDA stayed fixed. Recalibration

136   retained source CSP and refitted LDA with source training plus current support; full refitting up-

137   dated both stages using that same union. Validation and query identities were excluded from all

138   CPU fitting and parameter selection.


139   EEGNet used the frozen Braindecode architecture (F1=4, D=2, F2=8, dropout 0.5), Adam at learn-

140   ing rate 0.001, batch size 16 and a maximum of 100 epochs. Channel means and scales were fitted to

141   source training only. Source-validation cross entropy selected the earliest best epoch with patience

142   15; the complete selected state was restored. All three seeds (20261012, 20261013 and 20261014)

143   were retained for each participant. The 54 completed source fits used 2,871 epochs and 8,613 opti-

144   mizer steps. No source fit was repeated for query evaluation. Query inference used evaluation mode


                                                        7
145   with fixed parameters, batch-normalization buffers and normalization statistics, with zero optimizer

146   or support updates.

158   Endpoints, statistical units and original comparisons

159   Balanced accuracy (BA) was the mean of the two class recalls. The primary endpoint was each

160   participant’s mean BA over its five target sessions. The primary contrast remained full CSP/LDA

161   refitting minus source-only CSP/LDA in the fixed remaining 12 identities. For EEGNet, all three

162   seed scores were averaged within participant/session before the five target sessions were averaged

163   within participant. Source query was reported separately. Neither seeds, sessions nor trials increased

164   the independent participant count.


165   Balanced-accuracy and participant-mean reliability summaries used 10,000 participant resamples

166   with seed 42 for descriptive 95% percentile intervals; paired CPU contrasts resampled paired partici-

167   pant differences. The separately prespecified session-level drift associations used 2,000 participant-

168   block resamples with seed 42, retaining each sampled participant’s five sessions. Development and

169   remaining cohorts were reported separately; all-18 summaries were secondary. Cohort intervals

170   containing 50% were not converted into a new formal chance hypothesis test. Previously exposed

171   test results preclude describing subsequent same-dataset evaluations as untouched external confir-

172   mation. Intervals condition on the observed cohort, preprocessing and fitted models; they do not

173   estimate uncertainty from alternative training datasets or hyperparameter choices. No additional

174   EEGNet-versus-CPU significance test was introduced. Computational repetition and seeds do not

175   supply new participants.

176   Exploratory saved-prediction QC and influence analyses

177   After observing the original results, we wrote a separate sensitivity rule before computing the new

178   QC summaries. Saved predictions were joined to the existing fixed QC flag by exact trial iden-

179   tity, role, true label and inclusion status. The analysis used neither new EEG loading nor model

180   inference. We reported full and unflagged query BA, true-class counts and paired differences for

181   every participant/session, retaining empty or single-class rows as undefined. BA required both true

182   classes; the target endpoint required all five session BAs. We did not silently average fewer sessions

183   or count seeds as participants.


184   We separated the complete-case sensitivity from the original 12-person analysis and compared sub-

185   sets again within the same eligible participants. Participant bootstrap settings remained 10,000

186   draws and seed 42. Additional pooled-query BA is clearly labeled as a different endpoint. Leave-

187   one-participant-out means assessed influence without deleting any person from the reported pri-

188   mary result. These are post-observation descriptive checks, not a change to the main analysis,

189   new QC threshold, confirmatory test or evidence that unflagged data are artifact-free. The original

190   source/support/validation data and fitted models were not cleaned or retrained by this procedure.



191   Post-review paired statistical checks

192   In response to review, we added an exploratory check of four contrasts using the saved participant

193   endpoints: full refit minus frozen, full refit minus LDA-only and LDA-only minus frozen in the pre-

194   defined 12 participants, and full refit minus frozen in the ten participants eligible for the unflagged

195   sensitivity. We enumerated all 2𝑛 sign assignments and used the absolute mean paired difference

196   as the two-sided statistic. Differences were expressed in BA units; values within 10−12 of the ob-

197   served tail boundary were included. This reference distribution assumes independent participant

198   differences with sign symmetry about zero. It is not a test based on randomized assignment and is



                                                        10
199   not assumption-free population inference.


200   We report unadjusted values and Holm adjustment across these four checks. The rules were recorded

201   before this computation, after both the existing results and the review had been seen. Adjustment

202   within this family does not remove earlier test exposure or make the checks confirmatory. Original

203   effect estimates, bootstrap intervals, cohort membership and model outputs remain unchanged. S1

204   Text, S11 documents enumeration, rounding diagnostics and the distinction between this test and

205   the original percentile intervals.



206   Task measurements and development-only reliability

207   The descriptive drift analysis compared each target session with the independent source query.

208   A source-trained task axis was the normalized difference between class means in standardized

209   44-dimensional mu/beta log-power coordinates; one-dimensional Wasserstein distance measured

210   change along that fixed axis. Covariance-shape change was measured relative to a source-training

211   Riemannian reference in a fixed 21-dimensional common-average-reference basis, after trace nor-

212   malization and fixed shrinkage. This shape proxy does not measure all distributional changes or

213   absolute amplitude. Target labels did not define either drift predictor. The outcome was target-

214   minus-source frozen-decoder BA; target-labeled class summaries were retrospective descriptions.

215   These pooled associations combine within- and between-participant variation; all five changes for

216   a participant also share the same small source-query baseline. Participant- block resampling pre-

217   serves this clustering but does not separate those sources of variation or remove error in the shared

218   baseline.


219   The task-measurement reliability probes used only development source training and predeclared sup-

220   port. Fixed power, PCA and supervised CSP-score measurements were evaluated with source-run

221   grouping, support split halves and cyclic-label controls. The controls are phase-alignment sensi-



                                                       11
222   tivities, not formal permutation p values. Separately, the remaining-cohort drift associations were

223   pre-frozen descriptive analyses, not policy selection. No diagnostic selected an adaptation threshold

224   or used query outcomes to choose a policy.


225   Source-run probes trained on one 20-event source run and evaluated the other, then reversed the

226   roles. Learned transformations were fitted within each source training fold; a transform fitted on

227   all 40 events was not reused to score a held-out source run. The supervised diagnostic refitted both

228   CSP and LDA within each fold. For support descriptions, the existing 40-event source models and

229   source-fitted coordinate scales were held fixed. Each class was split into chronological halves within

230   the current support run, without query access. Vector cosines and signed source-score contrasts

231   summarized agreement between the halves; agreement did not itself establish a useful adaptation

232   decision.


233   Cyclic label controls retained the prescribed run grouping and enumerated label phases. They were

234   not random-label exchangeability tests. All 240 supervised CSP/LDA fold/rotation pipelines and

235   all associated controls were retained. Supplementary Methods S4–S6 define the coordinate scales,

236   half sizes, score orientation, control enumeration and handling of degenerate cases.

393   Discussion

394   Increment beyond established supervised recalibration

395   The component comparison is the main addition to this longitudinal resource. Under the same 19/20-

396   label support budget, full refitting exceeded LDA-only recalibration by a secondary mean of 5.47

397   points. This suggests that updating CSP can add value in this particular pipeline; the primary 7.42-

398   point contrast instead measures the total change from a frozen pipeline to full supervised refitting.

399   Neither identifies a physiological mechanism or establishes a universal recommendation to update.


400   The distinction from the acquisition study matters. Its GR and PAR frameworks both adapted co-

401   variance recentering online; PAR additionally updated Riemannian class prototypes using first-run



                                                      21
402   labels. Their group comparison does not estimate LDA-only updating versus a fully frozen decoder,

403   and a nonsignificant group difference is not evidence of equivalence. Its separate GR-CSP extension

404   also differs from our supervised component-refitting comparison [4,5]. Similarly, LDA adaptation

405   and EEG data-space adaptation update different parts of the decoding process [9,10]. Their results

406   are relevant background, not additional experiments or independent confirmation of our contrast.


407   Useful adaptation requires more than a cohort-average improvement. The observed task-score ori-

408   entation was inconsistent within some support runs, and the prespecified drift proxies had wide

409   associations with performance. No threshold, gate or new strategy was chosen from these data.

410   Alignment methods remain important comparators for a separately reviewed study [6,7]; they can-

411   not be retrospectively credited to this benchmark. Reliable individual update decisions would re-

412   quire a separately frozen, group-aware nested development evaluation against equal-label-budget

413   controls.



414   Window semantics and signal quality limit interpretation

415   The strongest qualification is that the recorded endpoint labels classify cue-anchored windows

416   in a motor-imagery experiment, not a verified sustained imagery segment. Cue perception, feed-

417   back, ocular/muscle activity and online termination-related phases could contribute. The recording-

418   boundary exclusion of 44 short events neither repairs nor estimates those contributions. A fu-

419   ture phase-specific analysis would first require matched original task-start/end markers and sam-

420   ple clocks, a preserved new eligibility ledger and a separately reviewed fixed window. It must not

421   search window positions for better scores or overwrite the present benchmark.


422   The amplitude sensitivity directly addresses saved decoding outcomes, unlike a power-only QC

423   description. Its positive complete-case contrasts and lack of a single-participant sign reversal narrow

424   some simple influence explanations. However, two of twelve primary participants lose a valid five-



                                                        22
425   session endpoint, conditioning on amplitude changes the sample and small class counts remain.

426   Flagged fit and validation examples still influence the stored models. These limitations preclude

427   labeling the results artifact-free or robust to all signal-quality problems. The substantially different

428   cue/task meanings also cannot be resolved by an amplitude threshold.



429   Neural baseline and statistical scope

430   EEGNet’s weak held-out source-session score is the first interpretive constraint. Its target score does

431   not by itself identify the effect of cross-session drift. Saved histories show training loss reduction

432   without consistent validation benefit, heterogeneous stopping and seed variation. These support a

433   limited small-source benchmark, without proving a unique cause or that deep learning is unsuitable

434   for this task. Parameter choice, additional source labels or alignment would be new experiments,

435   not permissible post-query fixes.


436   There are 18 published participant identities, 12 in the primary comparison and six in reliability

437   probes, not 54 independent participants. The development allocation preceded these results and

438   was retained even though it did not validate an update gate; its +3.25-point contrast was reported

439   separately, without pooling it into the primary analysis. The primary interval spans approximately

440   2.5 to 12.5 points and three participants had decreases. These data describe a cohort average but

441   provide little precision for deciding who should receive an update. Bootstrap intervals condition

442   on these participants, fixed windows and fitted models. Earlier test exposure makes follow-up

443   QC, neural comparisons and manuscript-driven summaries exploratory. Source metadata, protocol

444   membership and biological independence also have unresolved qualifications. Offline task-label

445   accuracy does not measure acquired BCI skill, human learning or clinical benefit [8].




                                                        23
446   Conclusions

447   Full CSP/LDA refitting improved the cohort-average endpoint under the specified support-label

448   budget, whereas LDA-only recalibration versus frozen decoding remained uncertain. The secondary

449   same-label-budget advantage over LDA-only updating and retained individual/session variation mo-

450   tivate measuring when adaptation is useful, but no reliable task-aware gate was validated. Weak in-

451   dependent source-query EEGNet performance precedes its weak target result. New QC sensitivity

452   is qualified by incomplete class coverage and the continued influence of flagged fitting data. The

453   present evidence supports transparent interpretation of released cue-anchored task-label windows,

454   not isolated sustained imagery, skill retention or clinical validity.
