Exported cue windows and unresolved within-trial task phases 230 The released intervals are cue-anchored exported task-label windows, rather than five seconds 231 certified to contain uninterrupted motor imagery. Three questions are distinct: whether all samples 232 of an interval are available, whether its start corresponds to the intended marker, and whether its 233 complete span lies within active imagery or feedback. The first is established for the 7,156 included 234 intervals. The documented cue convention supports the second at the derivative/export level. The 235 third remains unresolved at trial level. 236 The original authors’ codebook distinguishes class cue markers 769/770 from subsequent task-start 237 markers 7691/7701 [1]. The preserved official MOABB Kumar adapter maps the former pair to 238 left/right hand and declares an interval of [0,5] seconds. NEMAR identifies MOABB conversion 239 and preserves corresponding onset/sample/class rows. Its metadata reports MOABB 1.5.0dev0 , 240 but the exact historical converter commit/options and original GDF marker arrays have not been 12 241 independently replayed. A current adapter is evidence of the documented convention, not proof of 242 every historical conversion operation. 243 The original Methods describe one-second fixation, a 1.5-second class cue, then a five-second of- 244 fline task period or online feedback lasting at most seven seconds; a two-second result display and 245 1.5-second rest follow [2]. Online evidence-threshold crossing or timeout can terminate the active 246 period. Consequently, a nominal five-second interval beginning at the class cue contains cue pre- 247 sentation and at most the following 3.5 seconds of the task phase, assuming the described phase 248 schedule. Earlier online termination may introduce post-termination signal, but its presence and 249 duration cannot be assigned to individual intervals from the retained labels. We do not claim that 250 every online interval includes result display or rest. 251 The source codebook describes 32766 as recording boundary triggers and 1000 as trial/rest start. 252 For 7692/7702 it describes offline trial end and online timeout/incorrect-hit events. The online 253 7693/7703 description is literally “Cue Left/Right Delivered”; we do not silently reinterpret it as 254 a correct hit. The authors’ versioned task-bounded extraction function starts at 7691/7701 and ter- 255 minates at the subsequent 7692/7702 or 7693/7703. This supports the distinction between their 256 task-period markers and the class-cue export, without equating either annotation convention with 257 evidence of sustained imagery physiology. 258 The 360 saved event tables contain onset , duration , trial_type , value and sample , but not the 259 complete within-trial marker sequence. Each of the 44 partial intervals is the twentieth and final 260 retained class event of an online recording and ends at its previously audited EDF sample boundary. 261 Their identities, lengths and exclusion reasons remain unchanged. This demonstrates recording- 262 boundary truncation of the stored interval, not 44 verified early task terminations, and does not 263 by itself establish an indexing error. No retained interval overlaps the next retained class-event 264 onset; absence of this overlap still cannot demonstrate continuous imagery within a window. 13 265 S7.1.
