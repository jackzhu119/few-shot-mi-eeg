10   Abstract

11   Supervised recalibration can update a brain–computer interface classifier, its spatial filters, or both.

12   We compared these choices in 18 participant records across six sessions of Kumar2024 (NEMAR

13   nm000177/v1.0.5). A frozen protocol separated six development participants from a predefined 12-

14   participant primary cohort. Source-only CSP/LDA was compared with classifier-only recalibration

15   and full CSP/LDA refitting; the two update methods used the same 19 or 20 current-session support

16   labels. Always-frozen EEGNet retained three fixed seeds. The primary full-refit-minus-frozen con-

17   trast was 7.42 percentage points (95% participant-bootstrap interval 2.49–12.48). The secondary

18   same-label-budget full-refit-minus-LDA-only contrast was 5.47 points (2.17–8.91), whereas LDA-

19   only recalibration versus frozen decoding remained uncertain. Three of the 12 participants had de-

20   creases of 0.20–5.82 points. An exploratory amplitude-QC analysis retained complete five-session

21   endpoints for ten participants, with a full-minus-frozen contrast of 5.96 points (0.99–11.24); missing

22   classes and conditional selection limit this comparison. In the primary cohort, EEGNet source-query

23   balanced accuracy was 52.92% and target BA 51.58%. Weak discrimination was therefore already

24   present within the source session; the target score alone does not identify the contribution of cross-

25   session drift. Reliability probes did not establish a useful adaptation gate. The documented export

26   convention anchors five-second windows at the visual cue, but their trial-level phase composition

27   was not reconstructed. Full refitting improved the cohort average under the specified support-label

28   budget, with substantial individual variation. Prior test exposure makes the follow-up analyses ex-

29   ploratory. The findings concern released task-label windows, rather than isolated sustained imagery,

30   human skill retention or clinical efficacy.

31   Introduction

32   Longitudinal motor-imagery brain–computer interface (BCI) decoding must account for changes in

33   recording distributions and in the relation between signals and task labels. Nonstationarity, limited

34   calibration data and evaluation design remain recurring challenges [1]. Common spatial patterns

35   (CSP) and linear discriminant analysis (LDA) offer a conventional setting in which spatial repre-

36   sentation and classification can be updated separately [2,3]. A current-session label budget may

37   improve decoding, but it does not establish that every participant or session benefits from updating.


38   The acquisition study used incremental covariance recentering with a Riemannian minimum-

39   distance-to-mean classifier.   Its personally adjusted recentering framework added supervised

40   class-prototype updates during the first run of each session; a separate pseudo-online analysis

41   extended recentering to CSP and a linear discriminant classifier [4,5]. These procedures establish

42   important precedents for adaptation and session-start calibration, but differ from refitting CSP and

43   LDA on the same small labeled support set.


44   Our primary comparison asks how much full refitting improves decoding over a completely frozen

45   CSP/LDA pipeline when approximately 20 current-session labels are available. This total contrast

46   combines access to support labels with updates to both stages. The secondary comparison, full refit-

47   ting versus LDA-only recalibration, more directly addresses the additional contribution of updating

48   spatial filters because the two methods use identical labels. We retain this predefined hierarchy

49   rather than promote the secondary comparison after seeing its result. A further question is whether

50   the available task measurements can support reliable decisions about when to update.


51   Distribution alignment is a related but different strategy. Euclidean alignment and Riemannian

52   transfer frameworks reduce between-domain mismatch before or within decoding [6,7]. Such ap-

53   proaches are relevant alternatives, not baselines that were implemented here. A covariance distance

54   or task-score measurement alone neither reproduces those methods nor establishes that a policy


                                                      3
55   based on it improves decoding. Likewise, decoder accuracy is not interchangeable with a user’s

56   mental-imagery skill [8]. This secondary analysis evaluates stored task labels rather than the origi-

57   nal study’s online interventions or acquired skills.


58   Earlier work examined unsupervised adaptation of LDA in motor-imagery BCIs [9]. EEG data-

59   space adaptation instead estimated supervised or unsupervised channel-space transformations be-

60   fore applying an existing CSP/LDA decoder; one evaluated setting used the first 20 target trials for

61   adaptation [10]. These methods update different components and were not implemented as com-

62   parators here.


63   EEGNet supplies a compact neural reference [11], but identical query trials do not equalize source-

64   validation labels, target support or optimization. We retain every fixed seed and class-collapsed

65   outcome and examine saved learning histories to interpret this small-source setting. We also trace

66   the exported events to their task semantics: a complete data window is not automatically a complete

67   imagery phase. The study contributes a component-level baseline comparison and an assessment of

68   whether task measurements are adequate for individual update decisions. The reliability evidence

69   was insufficient to validate such a decision rule.

93   Exported windows, task-phase meaning and chronological partition

94   The frozen analysis identified 7,200 exported left/right event identities and used five-second inter-

95   vals beginning at their annotated onset. Forty-four intervals ended at the recording boundary before

96   five seconds; they were preserved and excluded without padding or replacement, leaving 7,156

97   complete windows. Completeness here refers to available recorded samples, not to five seconds of

98   continuous motor imagery.


99   The original author-supplied codebook defines 769/770 as left/right cue markers and distinct


                                                       5
100   7691/7701 markers as task-period starts [14]. The MOABB Kumar adapter [15] maps 769/770 to

101   left/right and declares [0,5] seconds; the derivative’s class annotations and fixed/clipped durations

102   agree with this documented export convention. The precise historical converter commit and

103   the original trial-level marker arrays were not independently replayed. The original Methods

104   describe a 1.5-second cue preceding feedback, with an offline task duration of five seconds and

105   online feedback lasting at most seven seconds, followed by result and rest. These sources support

106   a cue-anchored task-label export convention; they do not independently certify the phase

107   alignment of every analyzed trial. A nominal cue-anchored window includes the cue interval and

108   at most the subsequent 3.5 seconds; early online termination could introduce later phases. Their

109   trial-level proportions cannot be measured without the original marker sequence and a verified

110   clock mapping. We did not reconstruct these phases from labels or alter any window.


111   All 44 short intervals occur on the last event of an online recording and stop at the audited EDF end.

112   This is a recording-boundary relationship, not evidence that exactly 44 tasks ended early. Neither

113   variable task duration by itself nor this boundary check establishes a sample-indexing error. The

114   original results are retained as decoding of the released task-label windows. S1 Text gives the event

115   evidence and the minimum requirements for a future phase-specific amendment.


116   We inspected all 360 retained event tables and the saved EDF-annotation checks. They contain the

117   left/right annotation stream, without separate task-start or task-end fields. The complete NEMAR

118   manifest also publicly lists 360 original bar-task GDF files. Their embedded event arrays and their

119   trial/sample mapping to the derivative were not parsed or validated in this analysis. Thus the missing

120   phase information concerns the analyzed derivative, rather than a demonstrated absence from the

121   original public distribution (S1 Text, S7).

130   Preprocessing and fixed amplitude-QC flag

131   Each event independently underwent common-average referencing across 22 channels, fourth-order

132   8–30 Hz Butterworth zero-phase filtering and polyphase resampling from 512 to 128 Hz, yielding

133   22 × 640 float32 values in volts. These epoch-local operations learned no held-out statistics. Zero-

134   phase filtering defines an offline analysis; it neither proves real-time operation nor removes a cue

135   or feedback contribution. Native CPz reference remains a metadata declaration.


136   The existing 250 µV maximum raw channel peak-to-peak rule flagged 1,512/7,156 complete win-

137   dows. Every flagged complete window remained in the primary analysis. The rule is an amplitude

138   flag, not expert artifact certification. Signal timing and non-neural contributions are separate quali-

139   fications. No threshold or preprocessing parameter was selected from the new sensitivity results.

142   tures and norm_trace=False, followed by shrinkage LDA (solver=lsqr, automatic shrinkage).

143   One prespecified configuration was used. Source-only CSP and LDA stayed fixed. Recalibration

144   retained source CSP and refitted LDA with source training plus current support; full refitting up-

145   dated both stages using that same union. Validation and query identities were excluded from all

146   CPU fitting and parameter selection.

170   seed scores were averaged within participant/session before the five target sessions were averaged

171   within participant. Source query was reported separately. Neither seeds, sessions nor trials increased

184   Exploratory saved-prediction QC and influence analyses

185   After observing the original results, we wrote a separate sensitivity rule before computing the new

186   QC summaries. Saved predictions were joined to the existing fixed QC flag by exact trial iden-

187   tity, role, true label and inclusion status. The analysis used neither new EEG loading nor model

188   inference. We reported full and unflagged query BA, true-class counts and paired differences for

189   every participant/session, retaining empty or single-class rows as undefined. BA required both true

190   classes; the target endpoint required all five session BAs. We did not silently average fewer sessions

191   or count seeds as participants.


192   We separated the complete-case sensitivity from the original 12-person analysis and compared sub-

193   sets again within the same eligible participants. Participant bootstrap settings remained 10,000

194   draws and seed 42. Additional pooled-query BA is clearly labeled as a different endpoint. Leave-

195   one-participant-out means assessed influence without deleting any person from the reported pri-

196   mary result. These are post-observation descriptive checks, not a change to the main analysis,

197   new QC threshold, confirmatory test or evidence that unflagged data are artifact-free. The original

                                                       10
198   source/support/validation data and fitted models were not cleaned or retrained by this procedure.



199   Post-review paired statistical checks

200   In response to review, we added an exploratory check of four contrasts using the saved participant

201   endpoints: full refit minus frozen, full refit minus LDA-only and LDA-only minus frozen in the pre-

202   defined 12 participants, and full refit minus frozen in the ten participants eligible for the unflagged

203   sensitivity. We enumerated all 2𝑛 sign assignments and used the absolute mean paired difference

204   as the two-sided statistic. Differences were expressed in BA units; values within 10−12 of the ob-

205   served tail boundary were included. This reference distribution assumes independent participant

206   differences with sign symmetry about zero. It is not a test based on randomized assignment and is

207   not assumption-free population inference.


208   We report unadjusted values and Holm adjustment across these four checks. The rules were recorded

209   before this computation, after both the existing results and the review had been seen. Adjustment

210   within this family does not remove earlier test exposure or make the checks confirmatory. Original

211   effect estimates, bootstrap intervals, cohort membership and model outputs remain unchanged. S1

212   Text, S11 documents enumeration, rounding diagnostics and the distinction between this test and

213   the original percentile intervals.



214   Task measurements and development-only reliability

215   The descriptive drift analysis compared each target session with the independent source query.

216   A source-trained task axis was the normalized difference between class means in standardized

217   44-dimensional mu/beta log-power coordinates; one-dimensional Wasserstein distance measured

218   change along that fixed axis. Covariance-shape change was measured relative to a source-training

219   Riemannian reference in a fixed 21-dimensional common-average-reference basis, after trace nor-



                                                        11
220   malization and fixed shrinkage. This shape proxy does not measure all distributional changes or

221   absolute amplitude. Target labels did not define either drift predictor. The outcome was target-

222   minus-source frozen-decoder BA; target-labeled class summaries were retrospective descriptions.

223   These pooled associations combine within- and between-participant variation; all five changes for

224   a participant also share the same small source-query baseline. Participant- block resampling pre-

225   serves this clustering but does not separate those sources of variation or remove error in the shared

226   baseline.


227   The task-measurement reliability probes used only development source training and predeclared sup-

228   port. Fixed power, PCA and supervised CSP-score measurements were evaluated with source-run

229   grouping, support split halves and cyclic-label controls. The controls are phase-alignment sensi-

230   tivities, not formal permutation p values. Separately, the remaining-cohort drift associations were

231   pre-frozen descriptive analyses, not policy selection. No diagnostic selected an adaptation threshold

232   or used query outcomes to choose a policy.


233   Source-run probes trained on one 20-event source run and evaluated the other, then reversed the

234   roles. Learned transformations were fitted within each source training fold; a transform fitted on

235   all 40 events was not reused to score a held-out source run. The supervised diagnostic refitted both

236   CSP and LDA within each fold. For support descriptions, the existing 40-event source models and

237   source-fitted coordinate scales were held fixed. Each class was split into chronological halves within

238   the current support run, without query access. Vector cosines and signed source-score contrasts

239   summarized agreement between the halves; agreement did not itself establish a useful adaptation

240   decision.


241   Cyclic label controls retained the prescribed run grouping and enumerated label phases. They were

242   not random-label exchangeability tests. All 240 supervised CSP/LDA fold/rotation pipelines and

243   all associated controls were retained. Supplementary Methods S4–S6 define the coordinate scales,



                                                       12
244   half sizes, score orientation, control enumeration and handling of degenerate cases.

246   We examined all saved epoch-level training/validation histories, selected epochs, fixed seeds and

247   existing source/target query metrics without loading checkpoints or repeating inference. Training

248   losses were accumulated in training mode while parameters changed; validation losses were com-

249   puted after the epoch in evaluation mode. Their gap is not a calibrated measure of generalization.

250   Model-level summaries describe the fixed fits rather than increase the participant count.


251   OpenAI ChatGPT/Codex assisted with code, validation, saved-result aggregation, figures, source

252   and reference checks, manuscript drafting/editing and document exports. Checks included ex-

254   checks, a public-input replay of the text-only postprocessor and visual document/figure review.

255   These computational checks were themselves AI-assisted; they are not an independent human repli-

401   Discussion

402   Increment beyond established supervised recalibration

403   The component comparison is the main addition to this longitudinal resource. Under the same 19/20-

404   label support budget, full refitting exceeded LDA-only recalibration by a secondary mean of 5.47

405   points. This suggests that updating CSP can add value in this particular pipeline; the primary 7.42-

406   point contrast instead measures the total change from a frozen pipeline to full supervised refitting.

407   Neither identifies a physiological mechanism or establishes a universal recommendation to update.


408   The distinction from the acquisition study matters. Its GR and PAR frameworks both adapted co-

409   variance recentering online; PAR additionally updated Riemannian class prototypes using first-run

410   labels. Their group comparison does not estimate LDA-only updating versus a fully frozen decoder,

411   and a nonsignificant group difference is not evidence of equivalence. Its separate GR-CSP extension

412   also differs from our supervised component-refitting comparison [4,5]. Similarly, LDA adaptation

413   and EEG data-space adaptation update different parts of the decoding process [9,10]. Their results

414   are relevant background, not additional experiments or independent confirmation of our contrast.


415   Useful adaptation requires more than a cohort-average improvement. The observed task-score ori-

416   entation was inconsistent within some support runs, and the prespecified drift proxies had wide

417   associations with performance. No threshold, gate or new strategy was chosen from these data.

418   Alignment methods remain important comparators for a separately reviewed study [6,7]; they can-

419   not be retrospectively credited to this benchmark. Reliable individual update decisions would re-

420   quire a separately frozen, group-aware nested development evaluation against equal-label-budget

421   controls.




                                                      22
422   Window semantics and signal quality limit interpretation

423   The strongest qualification is that the recorded endpoint labels classify cue-anchored windows

424   in a motor-imagery experiment, not a verified sustained imagery segment. Cue perception, feed-

425   back, ocular/muscle activity and online termination-related phases could contribute. The recording-

426   boundary exclusion of 44 short events neither repairs nor estimates those contributions. A fu-

427   ture phase-specific analysis would first require matched original task-start/end markers and sam-

428   ple clocks, a preserved new eligibility ledger and a separately reviewed fixed window. It must not

429   search window positions for better scores or overwrite the present benchmark.


430   The amplitude sensitivity directly addresses saved decoding outcomes, unlike a power-only QC

431   description. Its positive complete-case contrasts and lack of a single-participant sign reversal narrow

432   some simple influence explanations. However, two of twelve primary participants lose a valid five-

433   session endpoint, conditioning on amplitude changes the sample and small class counts remain.

434   Flagged fit and validation examples still influence the stored models. These limitations preclude

435   labeling the results artifact-free or robust to all signal-quality problems. The substantially different

436   cue/task meanings also cannot be resolved by an amplitude threshold.



437   Neural baseline and statistical scope

438   EEGNet’s weak held-out source-session score is the first interpretive constraint. Its target score does

439   not by itself identify the effect of cross-session drift. Saved histories show training loss reduction

440   without consistent validation benefit, heterogeneous stopping and seed variation. These support a

441   limited small-source benchmark, without proving a unique cause or that deep learning is unsuitable

442   for this task. Parameter choice, additional source labels or alignment would be new experiments,

443   not permissible post-query fixes.


444   There are 18 published participant identities, 12 in the primary comparison and six in reliability


                                                        23
445   probes, not 54 independent participants. The development allocation preceded these results and

446   was retained even though it did not validate an update gate; its +3.25-point contrast was reported

447   separately, without pooling it into the primary analysis. The primary interval spans approximately

448   2.5 to 12.5 points and three participants had decreases. These data describe a cohort average but

449   provide little precision for deciding who should receive an update. Bootstrap intervals condition

450   on these participants, fixed windows and fitted models. Earlier test exposure makes follow-up

451   QC, neural comparisons and manuscript-driven summaries exploratory. Source metadata, protocol

452   membership and biological independence also have unresolved qualifications. Offline task-label

453   accuracy does not measure acquired BCI skill, human learning or clinical benefit [8].




454   Conclusions

455   Full CSP/LDA refitting improved the cohort-average endpoint under the specified support-label

456   budget, whereas LDA-only recalibration versus frozen decoding remained uncertain. The secondary

457   same-label-budget advantage over LDA-only updating and retained individual/session variation mo-

458   tivate measuring when adaptation is useful, but no reliable task-aware gate was validated. Weak in-

459   dependent source-query EEGNet performance precedes its weak target result. New QC sensitivity

460   is qualified by incomplete class coverage and the continued influence of flagged fitting data. The

461   present evidence supports transparent interpretation of released cue-anchored task-label windows,

462   not isolated sustained imagery, skill retention or clinical validity.
