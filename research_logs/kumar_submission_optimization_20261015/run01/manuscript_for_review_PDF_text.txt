Abstract

11

Supervised recalibration in motor-imagery brain–computer interfaces can update both a classifier

12

and its spatial representation, but a useful update decision also requires reliable task measurements.

13

We reanalyzed 18 published participant identities across six sessions of Kumar2024 (NEMAR

14

nm000177/v1.0.5). A frozen protocol compared source-only CSP/LDA, classifier-only recalibra-

15

tion with frozen CSP, and full CSP/LDA refitting using the same 19 or 20 current-session support

16

labels. An always-frozen EEGNet baseline retained three fixed seeds. The original primary con-

17

trast in 12 remaining participants favored full refitting over frozen decoding by 7.42 percentage

18

points (95% participant-bootstrap interval 2.49–12.48). The secondary same-label-budget contrast

19

favored full refitting over LDA-only updating by 5.47 points (2.17–8.91); it was not promoted to

20

a primary endpoint. Three individual decreases ranged from 0.20 to 5.82 points. An exploratory

21

amplitude-QC sensitivity retained a defined five-session endpoint for 10 of these participants; its

22

full-minus-frozen contrast was 5.96 points (0.99–11.24), with substantial missing-class and selec-

23

tion limitations. EEGNet source-query balanced accuracy (BA) was 52.92% and target BA 51.58%,

24

so weak target performance cannot chiefly be assigned to cross-session drift. Source and support

25

reliability probes did not validate an adaptation gate. Event-code tracing identified cue-anchored

26

exported five-second windows rather than verified five-second continuous imagery; online phase

27

composition remains unquantified. Full CSP/LDA refitting improved the cohort-average endpoint

28

under the specified support-label budget, whereas the LDA-only contrast remained uncertain. Prior

29

test exposure makes the follow-up analyses exploratory. These results concern released task-label

30

windows, not isolated imagery physiology, human skill retention or clinical efficacy.

Introduction

32

Longitudinal motor-imagery brain–computer interface (BCI) decoding must account for changes in

33

recording distributions and in the relation between signals and task labels. Nonstationarity, limited

34

calibration data and evaluation design remain recurring challenges [1]. Common spatial patterns

35

(CSP) and linear discriminant analysis (LDA) offer a conventional setting in which spatial repre-

36

sentation and classification can be updated separately [2,3]. A current-session label budget may

37

improve decoding, but it does not establish that every participant or session benefits from updating.

38

Kumar and colleagues already studied fixed and adaptive CSP in longitudinal BCI training, includ-

39

ing updates using labels from a session’s first run [4,5]. We therefore do not claim that recalibra-

40

tion, first-run calibration followed by later-run evaluation, or adaptive CSP is a new procedure. Our

41

narrower question is whether updating the spatial filters in addition to the classifier changes perfor-

42

mance when both updates receive the same approximately 20 target labels, and whether available

43

task measurements are reliable enough to inform an update decision. The original full-refit versus

44

frozen comparison remains primary; the full-refit versus classifier-only comparison is an explicitly

45

secondary, equal-label-budget contrast.

46

Distribution alignment is a related but different strategy. Euclidean alignment and Riemannian

47

transfer frameworks reduce between-domain mismatch before or within decoding [6,7]. Such ap-

48

proaches are relevant alternatives, not baselines that were implemented here. A covariance distance

49

or task-score measurement alone neither reproduces those methods nor establishes that a policy

50

based on it improves decoding. Likewise, decoder accuracy is not interchangeable with a user’s

51

mental-imagery skill [8]. This secondary analysis evaluates stored task labels rather than the origi-

52

nal study’s online interventions or acquired skills.

53

EEGNet supplies a compact neural reference [9], but identical query trials do not equalize source-

54

validation labels, target support or optimization. We retain every fixed seed and class-collapsed
3

55

outcome and examine saved learning histories to interpret this small-source setting. We also trace

56

the exported events to their task semantics: a complete data window is not automatically a complete

57

imagery phase. The resulting contribution is a bounded comparison of frozen and supervised con-

58

ventional baselines, measurement reliability and explicit sources of uncertainty, rather than a newly

59

validated task-aware gate.

Materials and methods

Data source, original study and ethical status

62

We used the released Kumar2024 derivative, NEMAR nm000177/v1.0.5 [10], associated with the

63

original study and its correction [4,5]. The original Methods report University of Texas at Austin

64

protocol 2020-03-0073 and written informed consent before the experiment. These statements were

65

checked against accessible indexed original Methods passages; downloading the complete original

66

XML was unsuccessful. They describe the original collection, not approval of this secondary anal-

67

ysis. No new participants were recruited and no intervention was performed. The responsible

68

institution’s determination for this secondary use remains pending confirmation by the author; no

69

approval, exemption, waiver or no-review conclusion is asserted for this project.

70

The preserved derivative contains 18 published participant identities, six sessions and 360 EDF

71

recordings. It is not a new recording collection or an independent reproduction of the acquisition

72

pipeline. Published identities are the statistical units; numerical duplicate checks do not indepen-

73

dently verify biological identity. Invalid age placeholders and unresolved original training- protocol

74

membership preclude demographic or intervention-subgroup claims.

Exported windows, task-phase meaning and chronological partition

76

The frozen analysis identified 7,200 exported left/right event identities and used five-second inter-

77

vals beginning at their annotated onset. Forty-four intervals ended at the recording boundary before

78

five seconds; they were preserved and excluded without padding or replacement, leaving 7,156

79

complete windows. Completeness here refers to available recorded samples, not to five seconds of

80

continuous motor imagery.

81

The original author-supplied codebook defines 769/770 as left/right cue markers and distinct

82

7691/7701 markers as task-period starts [11]. The preserved MOABB Kumar adapter [12] maps

83

769/770 to left/right and declares [0,5] seconds; the derivative’s class annotations and fixed/clipped

84

durations agree with this documented export convention. The precise historical converter commit

85

and the original trial-level marker arrays were not independently replayed. The original Methods

86

describe a 1.5-second cue preceding feedback, with an offline task duration of five seconds and

87

online feedback lasting at most seven seconds, followed by result and rest. Thus the documented

88

export chain supports cue-anchored task-label windows, not isolation of a continuous five-second

89

imagery phase. A nominal cue-anchored window includes the cue interval and at most the

90

subsequent 3.5 seconds; early online termination could introduce later phases. Their trial-level

91

proportions cannot be measured without the original marker sequence and a verified clock mapping.

92

We did not reconstruct these phases from labels or alter any window.

93

All 44 short intervals occur on the last event of an online recording and stop at the audited EDF end.

94

This is a recording-boundary relationship, not evidence that exactly 44 tasks ended early. Neither

95

variable task duration by itself nor this boundary check establishes a sample-indexing error. The

96

original results are retained as decoding of the released task-label windows. S1 Text gives the event

97

evidence and the minimum requirements for a future phase-specific amendment.

98

Development identities were sub-1, 4, 7, 10, 13 and 16; the remaining 12 formed the fixed primary

102

103

104

105

Preprocessing and fixed amplitude-QC flag

107

Each event independently underwent common-average referencing across 22 channels, fourth-order

108

8–30 Hz Butterworth zero-phase filtering and polyphase resampling from 512 to 128 Hz, yielding

109

22 × 640 float32 values in volts. These epoch-local operations learned no held-out statistics. Zero-

110

phase filtering defines an offline analysis; it neither proves real-time operation nor removes a cue

111

or feedback contribution. Native CPz reference remains a metadata declaration.

112

The existing 250 µV maximum raw channel peak-to-peak rule flagged 1,512/7,156 complete win-

113

dows. Every flagged complete window remained in the primary analysis. The rule is an amplitude

114

flag, not expert artifact certification. Signal timing and non-neural contributions are separate quali-

115

fications. No threshold or preprocessing parameter was selected from the new sensitivity results.

116

Decoders and information budgets

117

The CPU pipeline used four-component CSP with Ledoit–Wolf regularization, log mean-square fea-

118

tures and norm_trace=False, followed by shrinkage LDA (solver=lsqr, automatic shrinkage).

119

One prespecified configuration was used. Source-only CSP and LDA stayed fixed. Recalibration

120

retained source CSP and refitted LDA with source training plus current support; full refitting up-

6

121

dated both stages using that same union. Validation and query identities were excluded from all

122

CPU fitting and parameter selection.

123

EEGNet used the frozen Braindecode architecture (F1=4, D=2, F2=8, dropout 0.5), Adam at learn-

124

ing rate 0.001, batch size 16 and a maximum of 100 epochs. Channel means and scales were fitted

125

to source training only. Source-validation cross entropy selected the earliest best epoch with pa-

126

tience 15; the complete selected state was restored. All three seeds—20261012, 20261013 and

127

20261014— were retained for each participant. The 54 completed source fits used 2,871 epochs

128

and 8,613 optimizer steps. No source fit was repeated for query evaluation. Query inference used

129

evaluation mode with fixed parameters, batch-normalization buffers and normalization statistics,

130

with zero optimizer or support updates.

Endpoints, statistical units and original comparisons

143

Balanced accuracy (BA) was the mean of the two class recalls. The primary endpoint was each

144

participant’s mean BA over its five target sessions. The primary contrast remained full CSP/LDA

145

refitting minus source-only CSP/LDA in the fixed remaining 12 identities. For EEGNet, all three

146

seed scores were averaged within participant/session before the five target sessions were averaged

147

within participant. Source query was reported separately. Neither seeds, sessions nor trials increased

148

the independent participant count.

149

Balanced-accuracy and participant-mean reliability summaries used 10,000 participant resamples

8

150

with seed 42 for descriptive 95% percentile intervals; paired CPU contrasts resampled paired partici-

151

pant differences. The separately prespecified session-level drift associations used 2,000 participant-

152

block resamples with seed 42, retaining each sampled participant’s five sessions. Development and

153

remaining cohorts were reported separately; all-18 summaries were secondary. Cohort intervals

154

containing 50% were not converted into a new formal chance hypothesis test. Previously exposed

155

test results preclude describing subsequent same-dataset evaluations as untouched external confir-

156

mation. Intervals condition on the observed cohort, preprocessing and fitted models; they do not

157

estimate uncertainty from alternative training datasets or hyperparameter choices. No additional

158

EEGNet-versus-CPU significance test was introduced. Computational repetition and seeds do not

159

supply new participants.

Exploratory saved-prediction QC and influence analyses

161

After observing the original results, we wrote a separate sensitivity rule before computing the new

162

QC summaries. Saved predictions were joined to the existing fixed QC flag by exact trial iden-

163

tity, role, true label and inclusion status. The analysis used neither new EEG loading nor model

164

inference. We reported full and unflagged query BA, true-class counts and paired differences for

165

every participant/session, retaining empty or single-class rows as undefined. BA required both true

166

classes; the target endpoint required all five session BAs. We did not silently average fewer sessions

167

or count seeds as participants.

168

We separated the complete-case sensitivity from the original 12-person analysis and compared sub-

169

sets again within the same eligible participants. Participant bootstrap settings remained 10,000

170

draws and seed 42. Additional pooled-query BA is clearly labeled as a different endpoint. Leave-

171

one-participant-out means assessed influence without deleting any person from the reported pri-

172

mary result. These are post-observation descriptive checks, not a change to the main analysis,

173

new QC threshold, confirmatory test or evidence that unflagged data are artifact-free. The original
9

174

source/support/validation data and fitted models were not cleaned or retrained by this procedure.

Task measurements and development-only reliability

176

The descriptive drift analysis compared each target session with the independent source query.

177

A source-trained task axis was the normalized difference between class means in standardized

178

44-dimensional mu/beta log-power coordinates; one-dimensional Wasserstein distance measured

179

change along that fixed axis. Covariance-shape change was measured relative to a source-training

180

Riemannian reference in a fixed 21-dimensional common-average-reference basis, after trace nor-

181

malization and fixed shrinkage. This shape proxy does not measure all distributional changes or

182

absolute amplitude. Target labels did not define either drift predictor. The outcome was target-

183

minus-source frozen-decoder BA; target-labeled class summaries were explicitly retrospective de-

184

scriptions.

185

The task-measurement reliability probes used only development source training and predeclared sup-

186

port. Fixed power, PCA and supervised CSP-score measurements were evaluated with source-run

187

grouping, support split halves and cyclic-label controls. The controls are phase-alignment sensi-

188

tivities, not formal permutation p values. Separately, the remaining-cohort drift associations were

189

pre-frozen descriptive analyses, not policy selection. No diagnostic selected an adaptation threshold

190

or used query outcomes to choose a policy.

191

Source-run probes trained on one 20-event source run and evaluated the other, then reversed the

192

roles. Learned transformations were fitted within each source training fold; a transform fitted on

193

all 40 events was not reused to score a held-out source run. The supervised diagnostic refitted both

194

CSP and LDA within each fold. For support descriptions, the existing 40-event source models and

195

source-fitted coordinate scales were held fixed. Each class was split into chronological halves within

196

the current support run, without query access. Vector cosines and signed source-score contrasts

10

197

summarized agreement between the halves; agreement did not itself establish a useful adaptation

198

decision.

199

Cyclic label controls retained the prescribed run grouping and enumerated label phases. They were

200

not random-label exchangeability tests. All 240 supervised CSP/LDA fold/rotation pipelines and

201

all associated controls were retained. Supplementary Methods S4–S6 define the coordinate scales,

202

half sizes, score orientation, control enumeration and handling of degenerate cases.

203

Saved training review and AI-assisted preparation

204

We examined all saved epoch-level training/validation histories, selected epochs, fixed seeds and

205

existing source/target query metrics without loading checkpoints or repeating inference. Training

206

losses were accumulated in training mode while parameters changed; validation losses were com-

207

puted after the epoch in evaluation mode. Their gap is not a calibrated measure of generalization.

208

Model-level summaries describe the fixed fits rather than increase the participant count.

209

OpenAI ChatGPT/Codex assisted with code, validation, saved-result aggregation, figures, source

210

and reference checks, and manuscript drafting/editing. Checks included exact identity joins and

211

hashes, original-endpoint reconstruction, separately implemented arithmetic checks, a public-input

212

replay of the text-only postprocessor and visual document/figure review. These computational

213

checks were themselves AI-assisted; they are not an independent human replication or proof of

214

biological validity. The human author must personally review and approve the final methods, ref-

215

erences, results and interpretations before submission. The final approval and account of personal

216

checks remain author-provided facts.

Discussion

342

Increment beyond established supervised recalibration

343

The findings extend an existing longitudinal resource by separating two updates under the same

344

small current-session label allowance. The original study already used adaptive CSP and first-run

345

labels; reproducing that broad design is not a novel algorithm [4]. The secondary +5.47-point full-

346

versus-LDA-only contrast suggests that retaining the source spatial representation can limit this

347

conventional pipeline, even after classifier recalibration. It does not uniquely identify a physiolog-

348

ical mechanism, establish compute efficiency or replace the original primary comparison. Devel-

349

opment/remaining differences and individual variation also prevent a universal recommendation to

350

refit.

351

Useful adaptation requires more than a cohort-average improvement. The observed task-score ori-

352

entation was inconsistent within some support runs, and the prespecified drift proxies had wide

353

associations with performance. No threshold, gate or new strategy was chosen from these data.

354

Alignment methods remain important comparators for a separately reviewed study [6,7]; they can-

355

not be retrospectively credited to this benchmark. Reliable individual update decisions would re-

356

quire a separately frozen, group-aware nested development evaluation against equal-label-budget

357

controls.

358

Window semantics and signal quality limit interpretation

359

The strongest qualification is that the recorded endpoint labels classify cue-anchored windows

360

in a motor-imagery experiment, not a verified sustained imagery segment. Cue perception, feed-

361

back, ocular/muscle activity and online termination-related phases could contribute. The recording-

362

boundary exclusion of 44 short events neither repairs nor estimates those contributions. A fu-

363

ture phase-specific analysis would first require matched original task-start/end markers and sam21

364

ple clocks, a preserved new eligibility ledger and a separately reviewed fixed window. It must not

365

search window positions for better scores or overwrite the present benchmark.

366

The amplitude sensitivity directly addresses saved decoding outcomes, unlike a power-only QC

367

description. Its positive complete-case contrasts and lack of a single-participant sign reversal narrow

368

some simple influence explanations. However, two of twelve primary participants lose a valid five-

369

session endpoint, conditioning on amplitude changes the sample and small class counts remain.

370

Flagged fit and validation examples still influence the stored models. These limitations preclude

371

labeling the results artifact-free or robust to all signal-quality problems. The substantially different

372

cue/task meanings also cannot be resolved by an amplitude threshold.

373

Neural baseline and statistical scope

374

EEGNet’s weak held-out source-session score is the first interpretive constraint. Its target score

375

should not be presented as mainly a consequence of cross-session drift. Saved histories show train-

376

ing loss reduction without consistent validation benefit, heterogeneous stopping and seed variation.

377

These support a limited small-source benchmark, without proving a unique cause or that deep learn-

378

ing is unsuitable for this task. Parameter choice, additional source labels or alignment would be

379

new experiments, not permissible post-query fixes.

380

There are 18 published participant identities, 12 in the primary comparison and six in reliability

381

probes, not 54 independent participants. Bootstrap intervals condition on these participants, fixed

382

windows and fitted models. Earlier test exposure makes follow-up QC, neural comparisons and

383

manuscript-driven summaries exploratory. Source metadata, protocol membership and biological

384

independence also have unresolved qualifications. Offline task-label accuracy does not measure

385

acquired BCI skill, human learning or clinical benefit [8].

Conclusions

387

Full CSP/LDA refitting improved the cohort-average endpoint under the specified support-label

388

budget, whereas the LDA-only contrast remained uncertain. The secondary same-label-budget

389

advantage over LDA-only updating and retained individual/session variation motivate measuring

390

when adaptation is useful, but no reliable task-aware gate was validated. Weak independent source-

391

query EEGNet performance precedes its weak target result. New QC sensitivity is qualified by

392

incomplete class coverage and unchanged fitting artifacts. The present evidence supports transpar-

393

ent interpretation of released cue-anchored task-label windows, not isolated sustained imagery, skill

394

retention or clinical validity.
