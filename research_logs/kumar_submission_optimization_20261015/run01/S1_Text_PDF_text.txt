S7.

Exported cue windows and unresolved within-trial task

phases

229

The released intervals are cue-anchored exported task-label windows, rather than five seconds

230

certified to contain uninterrupted motor imagery. Three questions are distinct: whether all samples

231

of an interval are available, whether its start corresponds to the intended marker, and whether its

232

complete span lies within active imagery or feedback. The first is established for the 7,156 included

233

intervals. The documented cue convention supports the second at the derivative/export level. The

234

third remains unresolved at trial level.

235

The original authors’ codebook distinguishes class cue markers 769/770 from subsequent task-start

236

markers 7691/7701 [1]. The preserved official MOABB Kumar adapter maps the former pair to

237

left/right hand and declares an interval of [0,5] seconds. NEMAR identifies MOABB conversion

238

and preserves corresponding onset/sample/class rows. Its metadata reports MOABB 1.5.0dev0,

239

but the exact historical converter commit/options and original GDF marker arrays have not been

240

independently replayed. A current adapter is evidence of the documented convention, not proof of

241

every historical conversion operation.

242

The original Methods describe one-second fixation, a 1.5-second class cue, then a five-second of-

243

fline task period or online feedback lasting at most seven seconds; a two-second result display and

244

1.5-second rest follow [2]. Online evidence-threshold crossing or timeout can terminate the active

12

245

period. Consequently, a nominal five-second interval beginning at the class cue contains cue pre-

246

sentation and at most the following 3.5 seconds of the task phase, assuming the described phase

247

schedule. Earlier online termination may introduce post-termination signal, but its presence and

248

duration cannot be assigned to individual intervals from the retained labels. We do not claim that

249

every online interval includes result display or rest.

250

The source codebook describes 32766 as recording boundary triggers and 1000 as trial/rest start.

251

For 7692/7702 it describes offline trial end and online timeout/incorrect-hit events. The online

252

7693/7703 description is literally “Cue Left/Right Delivered”; we do not silently reinterpret it as

253

a correct hit. The authors’ versioned task-bounded extraction function starts at 7691/7701 and ter-

254

minates at the subsequent 7692/7702 or 7693/7703. This supports the distinction between their

255

task-period markers and the class-cue export, without equating either annotation convention with

256

evidence of sustained imagery physiology.

257

The 360 saved event tables contain onset, duration, trial_type, value and sample, but not the

258

complete within-trial marker sequence. Each of the 44 partial intervals is the twentieth and final

259

retained class event of an online recording and ends at its previously audited EDF sample boundary.

260

Their identities, lengths and exclusion reasons remain unchanged. This demonstrates recording-

261

boundary truncation of the stored interval, not 44 verified early task terminations, and does not

262

by itself establish an indexing error. No retained interval overlaps the next retained class-event

263

onset; absence of this overlap still cannot demonstrate continuous imagery within a window.

264

The minimum evidence for a future phase-specific amendment is the original bar-task POS/TYP

265

marker arrays for all 360 runs, their sample rate and sample-origin convention, and a pinned con-

266

verter or maintainer-confirmed mapping to the current trial IDs. Cue, task start, outcome/task end

267

and recording boundaries must be matched without consulting decoding outcomes. Before any new

268

evaluation, an amendment would need to specify one anchor and duration, handling of early ends

13

269

270

271

272

these marker definitions [3].
